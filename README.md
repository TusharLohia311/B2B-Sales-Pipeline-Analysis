## Project Overview

This project presents an end-to-end analysis of a B2B sales pipeline using Microsoft Excel. The analysis covers data quality assessment, data cleaning, exploratory data analysis (EDA), KPI development, sales performance analysis, an interactive dashboard, and business insights.

The objective is to understand pipeline performance, deal-value patterns, product and regional revenue distribution, sales-cycle behavior, and revenue concentration in order to support data-driven sales decisions.

---

## Business Problem

A B2B sales organization needs to understand:

- How efficiently opportunities move through the sales pipeline
- What proportion of opportunities are won
- How much revenue is generated from Won deals
- How deal values are distributed
- Which products contribute most to Won revenue
- How Won revenue varies by region and month
- Whether revenue is concentrated among a smaller group of deals
- Where unusual product, agent, or regional patterns may require further investigation

## Key KPIs

| KPI | Result |
|---|---:|
| Win Rate | **63.15%** |
| Total Won Revenue | **₹10,005,534** |
| Median Won Deal Value | **₹1,117** |
| Top 10% Revenue Share | **27.58%** |
| Average Sales Cycle | **51.78 days** |

### KPI Definitions

- **Win Rate:** Won deals ÷ (Won + Lost deals)
- **Total Won Revenue:** Sum of `close_value` for Won deals
- **Median Won Deal Value:** Median `close_value` among Won deals
- **Top 10% Revenue Share:** Revenue generated by the top 10% of Won deals ÷ total Won revenue
- **Average Sales Cycle:** Average Sales Cycle Length for Won deals

## Business Insights

### 1. Won Deal Values Are Highly Right-Skewed

The average Won deal value is **₹2,360.91**, compared with a median of **₹1,117**.

This difference indicates that a smaller number of larger deals raise the average above the typical Won deal value. The median therefore provides useful context when evaluating the typical deal size.

### 2. Won Revenue Is Concentrated Across a Few Products

The three largest contributors to Won revenue are:

| Product | Won Revenue |
|---|---:|
| GTX Pro | ₹3.51M |
| GTX Plus Pro | ₹2.63M |
| MG Advanced | ₹2.22M |

The revenue portfolio is therefore not evenly distributed across products, making the performance of leading products important to monitor.

### 3. A Smaller Group of Won Deals Generates a Significant Share of Revenue

The top 10% of Won deals generate **27.58% of total Won revenue**, while the remaining 90% generate **72.42%**.

This highlights the importance of evaluating both deal volume and deal value rather than relying on opportunity counts alone.

### 4. High-Value GTK 500 Deals Show a Concentration Pattern

All **15 Won deals above ₹10,000** in the analysis are GTK 500 deals. These high-value deals are concentrated across the West region and three sales agents.

This is a concentration pattern that may warrant further investigation. It does not establish that the product, region, or agents caused the higher deal values.

### 5. East Region Shows a Higher Share of Higher-Value Products

The East region has a **50.95% share of higher-value products**, compared with approximately **41%** in the Central and West regions.

The East region also has an average Won deal value of **₹2,639.28**.

This represents an association worth investigating further rather than evidence of a causal relationship.

## Business Recommendations

### 1. Increase the Value of Smaller Deals

Analyze smaller Won deals for upsell and cross-sell opportunities. Identify products, customer characteristics, and deal patterns associated with larger deal values.

### 2. Monitor Core Product Revenue Concentration

Track GTX Pro, GTX Plus Pro, and MG Advanced closely because they account for a substantial portion of Won revenue. Monitoring concentration can help identify potential portfolio risks.

### 3. Investigate the GTK 500 Concentration Pattern

Examine customer characteristics, deal composition, sales process, region, and agent-level factors behind the 15 high-value GTK 500 Won deals.

### 4. Investigate the East Region's Product Mix

Study whether the East region's higher share of higher-value products is associated with identifiable customer, product, or sales-process characteristics. Any successful patterns can then be evaluated for applicability elsewhere.

## Analysis Limitations

The analysis includes several limitations:

1. **500 opportunities have missing Engage Dates.**
2. **Date-parsing ambiguity exists for dates where the day is ≤12**, meaning some source-date interpretations could not be determined with complete certainty.
3. **513 potential near-duplicate records were flagged and retained** because they could not be confidently classified as duplicates.
4. Some deep-dive findings are based on relatively small samples, such as the 15 high-value GTK 500 Won deals.
5. Observed associations should not be interpreted as evidence of causation.
